Part 2: Simulation via numerical integration

Here is scene/pinned2.json with varying parameters:

The spring constant ks defines the "stiffness" of the fabric. Low ks (e.g., 1 N/m on the right): The cloth appears very stretchy and loose, and is able to deform a lot. As it falls, there is significant sagging between the pinned points, hence the large arc. The folds are small and numerous because the material lacks the internal tension to resist gravity effectively. High ks (e.g., 10,000 N/m on the left): The cloth behaves more like heavy denim, and doesn't deform much. It resists stretching strongly. In the final resting state, the top edge between pinned corners remains much flatter, and the folds are larger and more "structured."

Density determines the mass of each point mass (m = total_mass/num_masses). Low Density (1 g/cm^2 on the right): The cloth is light as it has a very low total mass. Because the gravitational force (F = ma) is smaller, the internal spring forces easily counteract gravity. The cloth reaches a resting state quickly with minimal sagging, as there is less force pulling down between the pinned points. High Density (1000 g/cm^2 on the left): The cloth is heavy. The increased mass exerts more downward force on the springs. Even with a standard ks, a high-density cloth will sag significantly, as the weight of the "fabric" overcomes the spring tension.

Damping simulates energy loss (friction, air resistance, internal heat). Low Damping (e.g., 0% on the right): The cloth is extremely "bouncy." When dropped, it will oscillate back and forth for a very long time before coming to rest. It may even appear jittery or chaotic as energy is not being dissipated from the system. High Damping (e.g., 100% on the left): The motion appears "thick," as if the cloth is moving through honey. When dropped, it falls very slowly and settles into its resting position almost immediately without any swinging or bouncing.

Part 3: Handling collisions with other objects

How did we implement the handling of collisions with spheres and planes:

Sphere Collisions

If a point mass is inside the sphere (distance to origin < radius), it gets bumped to the surface by projecting the direction vector from the origin to the point mass onto the sphere surface (normalize × radius). The new position is last_position + (1 - friction) × (tangent_point - last_position).

Plane Collisions

If a point mass crosses the plane (signed distances on opposite sides), the tangent point is found by linearly interpolating between last and current positions at the crossing. A small SURFACE_OFFSET keeps it just above the surface. The new position is last_position + (1 - friction) × (tangent_point + offset - last_position).

Integration in simulate

After Verlet integration and Provot constraint correction, every point mass is tested against every collision object via co->collide(pm), which adjusts its position in place if needed.

Difference between results of the 3 ks values:

Higher ks means stiffer springs, so the cloth behaves differently across the three values:

  • ks = 500: The cloth is very soft and drapes closely over the sphere, with many folds and the edges hanging low.
  • ks = 5000: A moderate drape — the cloth conforms to the sphere while retaining some stiffness, with fewer folds.
  • ks = 50000: The cloth is very stiff and barely conforms to the sphere, sitting almost like a rigid sheet on top with minimal folding and edges barely drooping.
